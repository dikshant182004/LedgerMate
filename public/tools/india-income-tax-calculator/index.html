New Tax Regime Slab Structure (FY 2025–26 / AY 2026–27)

The revised New Tax Regime features six progressive tax brackets: 0 to ₹3,00,000 (Nil), ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 (5%), ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 (10%), ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 (15%), ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000 (20%), and Above ₹15,00,000 (30%). In addition, a 4% Health & Education Cess is levied on total tax payable.

Section 87A Tax Rebate (Zero Tax up to ₹7.75 Lakhs)

Under Section 87A of the Income Tax Act, resident individuals with total taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 in the New Regime receive a 100% tax rebate of up to ₹25,000. When combined with the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried personnel, any gross salary up to ₹7,75,000 incurs absolutely ₹0 tax liability.

₹75,000 Standard Deduction for Salaried Employees

Union Budget 2024 increased the Standard Deduction from ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 exclusively for taxpayers opting for the New Tax Regime. Pensioners also receive an increased family pension deduction of ₹25,000 (up from ₹15,000). Taxpayers under the Old Tax Regime remain restricted to the prior ₹50,000 standard deduction.

Old Tax Regime Chapter VI-A Deductions

The Old Regime allows taxpayers to reduce taxable income using itemized investments: Section 80C (up to ₹1,50,000 for EPF, PPF, ELSS, Life Insurance, principal repayment of home loans), Section 80D (₹25,000 for self/family health insurance, plus ₹50,000 for senior citizen parents), Section 24(b) (up to ₹2,00,000 home loan interest), and Section 10(13A) House Rent Allowance (HRA).

The Mathematical Breakeven Threshold

To determine if the Old Regime saves more money, calculate your total eligible deductions. For income between ₹10 Lakhs and ₹15 Lakhs, your total deductions must exceed ₹3,75,000 to ₹4,25,000 to overcome the lower tax slab rates of the New Regime. If your deductions are below this threshold, the New Regime is mathematically superior.

Comprehensive Analysis: New vs Old Tax Regime for FY 2025–26 (AY 2026–27)

Navigating the Indian direct taxation landscape requires a clear understanding of the dual tax structure introduced by Section 115BAC of the Income Tax Act. For the assessment year 2026–27, the New Tax Regime continues as the default taxation scheme for all individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and Associations of Persons (AOPs). Taxpayers retain the freedom to opt for the Old Tax Regime if their deduction profile warrants it.

1. The New Tax Regime Slabs and Relief Measures

The revised New Tax Regime aims to simplify tax compliance by eliminating paperwork and investment proof verification while providing lower marginal rates:

Taxable Income Slab Tax Rate Tax on Slabs Cumulative Tax
Up to ₹3,00,000 0% ₹0 ₹0
₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 5% ₹20,000 ₹20,000 (Rebate applies if total ≤ ₹7L)
₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 10% ₹30,000 ₹50,000
₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 15% ₹30,000 ₹80,000
₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000 20% ₹60,000 ₹1,40,000
Above ₹15,00,000 30% 30% of excess ₹1,40,000 + 30% on excess

2. Detailed Tax Comparison Across Salary Brackets

To see how the two regimes perform across standard income benchmarks, review the comparative tax liabilities below (assuming typical Old Regime deductions of ₹1,50,000 under 80C, ₹25,000 under 80D, and ₹1,20,000 HRA exemption):

  • ₹7,75,000 Annual Salary: Under the New Regime, standard deduction of ₹75,000 reduces taxable income to ₹7,00,000. Full Section 87A rebate applies, resulting in ₹0 tax. Under the Old Regime, tax would be approximately ₹25,480. (New Regime saves ₹25,480).
  • ₹10,00,000 Annual Salary: New Regime tax is ₹44,200 (including 4% cess). Old Regime tax with standard deductions is ₹53,560. (New Regime saves ₹9,360).
  • ₹15,00,000 Annual Salary: New Regime tax is ₹1,30,000. Old Regime tax is ₹1,57,560. (New Regime saves ₹27,560).
  • ₹20,00,000 Annual Salary: New Regime tax is ₹2,75,600. Old Regime tax is ₹3,13,560. (New Regime saves ₹37,960).
  • ₹30,00,000 Annual Salary: New Regime tax is ₹5,87,600. Old Regime tax is ₹6,25,560. (New Regime saves ₹37,960).

3. The Breakeven Threshold: When Does the Old Regime Win?

The Old Tax Regime becomes advantageous only when your total aggregate deductions exceed the breakeven points tabulated below:

Annual Gross Income Required Deductions to Break Even Recommended Regime for Most People
₹8,00,000 to ₹10,00,000 ₹2,50,000 to ₹3,00,000 New Tax Regime
₹10,00,000 to ₹15,00,000 ₹3,75,000 to ₹4,00,000 New Tax Regime (unless high rent + home loan)
₹15,00,000 to ₹25,00,000 ₹4,25,000 to ₹4,50,000 New Tax Regime
Above ₹50,00,000 ₹4,75,000+ New Tax Regime (due to lower 25% surcharge cap)

4. Surcharges for High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs)

For taxpayers with total annual income exceeding ₹50 Lakhs, surcharge applies on top of basic tax rates:

  • ₹50 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore: 10% surcharge under both regimes.
  • ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crores: 15% surcharge under both regimes.
  • ₹2 Crores to ₹5 Crores: 25% surcharge under both regimes.
  • Above ₹5 Crores: Capped at 25% under the New Tax Regime, whereas the Old Tax Regime levied up to 37% surcharge (bringing the peak marginal rate above 42.7%). This makes the New Regime substantially more attractive for top-bracket earners.

5. Switching Regimes: Salaried vs Business Income

If you are a salaried individual without business earnings (filing ITR-1 or ITR-2), you can elect your preferred regime every year. You can declare one regime to your employer at the beginning of the financial year for TDS deduction purposes, and choose a different regime when filing your final return with the Income Tax Department if your investment deductions change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is income up to ₹7.75 Lakh completely tax-free in the New Tax Regime?

Yes. For salaried individuals in the New Tax Regime for FY 2025-26, the ₹75,000 standard deduction lowers taxable income to ₹7,00,000. Under Section 87A, taxable income up to ₹7 Lakh receives a full tax rebate of up to ₹25,000, resulting in zero net tax liability.

When is the Old Tax Regime better than the New Regime?

The Old Regime is typically better only if your total eligible tax deductions (Section 80C ₹1.5L, 80D health insurance ₹25k-₹50k, HRA rent exemption, and home loan interest under Section 24b up to ₹2L) exceed ₹3.75 Lakh to ₹4.25 Lakh per year.

What is the Standard Deduction for FY 2025-26?

The Standard Deduction for salaried employees and pensioners under the New Tax Regime is ₹75,000 (increased from ₹50,000 in Budget 2024). Under the Old Tax Regime, the Standard Deduction remains ₹50,000.

Can I switch between New and Old Tax Regime every year?

Salaried individuals without business income can freely switch between the New and Old Tax Regimes each financial year when filing their Income Tax Return (ITR-1 or ITR-2). However, individuals with business or professional income (ITR-3 or ITR-4) can opt out of the New Regime only once in their lifetime.

Is employer NPS contribution deductible under the New Tax Regime?

Yes, employer contribution to the National Pension System (NPS) under Section 80CCD(2) up to 14% of Basic for central government employees and 10% for private sector employees is deductible under both New and Old regimes.

What are the surcharge rates for high earners in India?

Under the New Tax Regime, surcharge is capped at 25% for income exceeding ₹2 Crore (10% for ₹50L-₹1Cr, 15% for ₹1Cr-₹2Cr). Under the Old Regime, the surcharge previously reached up to 37% for income above ₹5 Crore.

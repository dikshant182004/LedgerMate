How to Split a Restaurant Bill Fairly

Splitting a restaurant check should never cause friction between friends. Depending on the size of your group and what people ordered, here are the three most common ways to split a check:

1. The Straight Even Split (Best for casual meals & shared plates)

Divide the final check (subtotal + tax + tip) by the number of diners. This is the fastest, least stressful method when everyone ordered roughly the same price range or shared tapas, pizzas, and family-style platters.

2. The Non-Drinker / Itemized Split

Cocktails and wine frequently double the check. If two people had multiple $18 cocktails while others only had water, subtract the alcohol subtotal and its proportionate tip/tax. The non-drinkers pay for their food plus food tax/tip, and the drinkers split the alcohol total.

3. Tip on Subtotal vs. Tip on Total

Etiquette experts universally agree: you only need to tip on the pre-tax subtotal of food and services. Paying tip on top of government sales tax means you are tipping on a tax, which is unnecessary though appreciated by servers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should tip be calculated before or after tax?

Standard restaurant etiquette dictates that tips should be calculated on the pre-tax subtotal of food and drinks, not on the sales tax.

How do you split a bill when someone didn't drink alcohol?

Subtract the drinks from the subtotal. Split the food evenly among all guests, and have only the drinkers cover the beverage subtotal with tip.

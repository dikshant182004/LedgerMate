Bachelor & Bachelorette Party Etiquette Guide

Organizing a bachelor or bachelorette party is one of the most rewarding parts of wedding season, but cost friction between attendees can strain friendships if not addressed upfront.

1. Who Pays for What?

By modern etiquette, the guests jointly cover the bride or groom’s lodging, dinners, drinks, and celebratory activities. The honoree should not have to reach into their wallet during the weekend. However, the honoree usually books and pays for their own plane ticket unless the bridal party collectively decides to gift the airfare.

2. Set the Budget Before Booking Non-Refundable Items

Always send an anonymous survey or group chat poll asking for maximum comfortable budgets before putting down a deposit on a multi-thousand-dollar luxury rental. Be transparent about estimated cost-per-person ranges.

3. Use LedgerMate for Settle-Ups (Avoid Venmo Spreadsheet Chaos)

Multiple bridesmaids or groomsmen will purchase different items: one puts down the Airbnb deposit, another buys the alcohol, someone else orders custom t-shirts. LedgerMate automatically calculates the minimum number of direct transfers so nobody overpays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the bride or groom pay for their bachelorette party?

Traditional etiquette is that the honoree (bride or groom) does not pay for lodging, dinners, drinks, or group activities during the party. Those costs are divided evenly among the attendees.

What if someone can only come for one day of the weekend?

Calculate their per-night lodging rate and only bill them for meals and activities they actually attended, rather than an even full-weekend split.

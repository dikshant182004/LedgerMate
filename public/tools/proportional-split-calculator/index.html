The Math of Proportional Bill Splitting

When two people live together with different salaries, splitting everything 50/50 might seem egalitarian on paper, but it produces drastically unequal outcomes. For example, if rent is $3,000, paying $1,500 represents 30% of a $5,000 income, but 60% of a $2,500 income.

The Formula: Each Person's Share = (Individual Income ÷ Total Combined Income) × Total Shared Expenses

By contributing in proportion to what you earn, both partners spend the exact same percentage of their paychecks on living essentials, leaving both individuals with fair discretionary spending power.

What Should Be Included in Joint Expenses?

  • Include: Rent/mortgage, electricity, gas, water, internet, shared groceries, home supplies, and joint streaming services.
  • Keep Separate: Student loans, personal credit card debt, personal car payments, individual clothing, and personal hobbies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 50/50 or proportional splitting better for couples?

Proportional splitting is widely recommended when incomes differ by more than 20%, as it avoids burnout and financial resentment.

Should we use net income or gross income?

Net income (take-home pay) is more accurate because tax brackets and deductions alter actual cash flow.

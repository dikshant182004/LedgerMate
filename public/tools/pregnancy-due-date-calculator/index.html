The Complete Medical Guide to Due Dates, Gestation & Trimesters

Pregnancy dating forms the foundation of all prenatal care—governing the timing of genetic screenings, ultrasound anatomy scans, glucose tolerance tests, and labor induction recommendations. Below is a comprehensive guide explaining clinical dating protocols.

1. What is Naegele’s Rule?

Formulated by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele in the 19th century, Naegele’s Rule is the international medical standard for estimating delivery dates:

Estimated Due Date = First Day of LMP + 1 Year - 3 Months + 7 Days
(Equivalently: First Day of LMP + 280 Days / 40 Completed Weeks)

Cycle-Length Compensation: Naegele's rule inherently assumes a textbook 28-day cycle with ovulation occurring on Day 14. If your cycle averages 32 days, your ovulation occurred approximately 4 days later (around Day 18); our calculator accounts for this by adding 4 days to your due date, preventing premature labor induction anxiety.

2. Ultrasound Dating vs. LMP Dating (ACOG Guidelines)

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) provides precise rules for reconciling LMP calculations with ultrasound scans:

  • Crown-Rump Length (CRL) up to 8 6/7 weeks: If ultrasound differs from LMP by more than 5 days, the ultrasound date replaces the LMP date.
  • 9 0/7 weeks to 13 6/7 weeks: If ultrasound differs by more than 7 days, ultrasound dating takes precedence.
  • Second Trimester (14 to 21 6/7 weeks): Ultrasound variance threshold widens to 10 to 14 days, because biological growth variations between individual fetuses begin to emerge.

3. Dating an IVF Pregnancy (Day 3 vs. Day 5 Transfer)

In Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) such as In Vitro Fertilization, the embryonic age is known with exact precision:

  • Day 5 Blastocyst: The blastocyst is already 5 days post-fertilization (gestational equivalent of Day 19). The estimated due date is calculated as Transfer Date + 261 Days.
  • Day 3 Cleavage-Stage Embryo: The embryo is 3 days post-fertilization (gestational equivalent of Day 17). Due date is calculated as Transfer Date + 263 Days.

5. Official Definitions of Pregnancy Term

To avoid complications from early elective deliveries, ACOG and the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM) established official terminology:

  • Preterm: Delivered before 37 weeks 0 days.
  • Early Term: 37 weeks 0 days through 38 weeks 6 days (babies delivered early term have slightly higher respiratory morbidity than full-term babies).
  • Full Term: 39 weeks 0 days through 40 weeks 6 days (optimal perinatal outcomes).
  • Late Term: 41 weeks 0 days through 41 weeks 6 days.
  • Postterm: 42 weeks 0 days and beyond (monitoring or induction required due to placental aging).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is pregnancy considered 40 weeks if it lasts 9 months?

A calendar month averages 30.4 days (roughly 4.34 weeks), not 4 weeks. 40 weeks equals 280 days, which is approximately 9 calendar months and 7 days. Furthermore, gestational age begins on Day 1 of your LMP—approximately two weeks before conception actually occurs—meaning you are already considered "2 weeks pregnant" at fertilization.

What if I have an irregular cycle or don't know my LMP?

If your periods are highly irregular or your last menstrual date is uncertain, an early transvaginal obstetric ultrasound (dating scan) between 7 and 11 weeks provides the most reliable date. By measuring the fetus's crown-rump length (CRL), gestational age can be established within a margin of 3 to 5 days.

Can my due date change during prenatal care?

Once a firm clinical due date is established via early ultrasound or accurate LMP, it is rarely changed. Later ultrasounds in the second and third trimesters measure fetal growth rather than gestational age, because biological genetic differences (some babies naturally smaller or larger) make late dating unreliable.

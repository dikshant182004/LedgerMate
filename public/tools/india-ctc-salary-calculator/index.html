Cost to Company (CTC) vs In-Hand Take-Home

CTC represents the total financial cost your employer spends to employ you annually. It includes components you never see in your monthly bank statement: Employer PF contribution (12%), Gratuity provision (4.81%), group medical insurance premiums, and variable annual bonuses. Your actual in-hand take-home salary is what remains after subtracting both employer overheads and statutory employee deductions (Employee EPF, state professional tax, and monthly TDS).

New vs Old Tax Regime Slabs (Budget 2024–2026)

The New Tax Regime is the default regime for salaried individuals. It features streamlined tax brackets (0 to ₹3L: 0%, ₹3L–₹7L: 5%, ₹7L–₹10L: 10%, ₹10L–₹12L: 15%, ₹12L–₹15L: 20%, Above ₹15L: 30%) with an increased standard deduction of ₹75,000 and Section 87A rebate making income up to ₹7.75 Lakhs completely tax-free. The Old Regime is only superior if you have eligible deductions exceeding ₹3.75 Lakhs to ₹4.0 Lakhs (combining Section 80C, 80D, HRA, and home loan interest under Section 24b).

EPF (12%) & Gratuity Mechanics

The Employee Provident Fund (EPF) mandates that 12% of your Basic Salary is deducted from your gross pay, while the employer contributes another 12% (3.67% to EPF + 8.33% to EPS pension scheme). Employees whose Basic exceeds ₹15,000/month may mutually opt with their employer to cap contributions at ₹1,800/month. Gratuity is budgeted at 4.81% of your Basic salary, but is legally claimable only after completing 5 full years of continuous service under the Payment of Gratuity Act.

HRA Exemption Formula (Old Tax Regime)

Under the Old Tax Regime, House Rent Allowance (HRA) is exempt from income tax under Section 10(13A) up to the minimum of three values: 1) Actual HRA received from employer, 2) Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic salary, or 3) 50% of Basic salary (for metro cities: Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai) or 40% of Basic (non-metros). Note: HRA exemption cannot be claimed under the New Tax Regime.

Understanding Why CTC Differs From In-Hand Take-Home Pay in India

Cost to Company (CTC) is the total annual financial package an employer spends to maintain an employee. A ubiquitous shock for fresh graduates and experienced professionals across Bangalore, Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Gurgaon is finding that their monthly bank deposit is 15% to 25% lower than their CTC offer letter divided by 12. Understanding how Indian payroll is structured enables you to negotiate salary packages effectively and structure allowances to optimize cash flow.

1. The Core Components of an Indian Salary Slip

A typical Indian salary structure consists of four major categories:

  • Basic Salary: The core foundation of your compensation, usually set at 40% to 50% of total CTC. Your Employee Provident Fund (EPF), Gratuity, and HRA are legally indexed directly to Basic Salary. A higher Basic increases your long-term retirement savings and tax deductions, but reduces your immediate liquidity.
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA): Designed to cover residential rental expenses. Typically set at 40% (non-metro) or 50% (metro) of Basic Salary. Under the Old Tax Regime, HRA is partially or fully exempt from income tax under Section 10(13A).
  • Special Allowance: A flexible balancing allowance used by HR departments to match the negotiated CTC figure. Unlike Basic Salary, Special Allowance does not attract additional EPF or gratuity contributions and is fully taxable as income.
  • Employer Retirement Contributions: Both the Employer's share of EPF (12% of Basic) and Gratuity provisioning (4.81% of Basic) are bundled inside your annual CTC quote, even though they represent deferred or contingent benefits that are never paid out as monthly cash.

2. New vs Old Tax Regime: Which Should You Choose in 2026?

Since Budget 2023 and the 2024 revisions, the New Tax Regime is the default regime in India. It replaces the complex web of exemptions with significantly reduced slab rates and a higher Standard Deduction:

3. The Exact Math: From 12 LPA CTC to Monthly Bank Deposit

Let us walk through a real-world example of a ₹12,00,000 CTC offer in Karnataka with 50% Basic:

  • Annual CTC: ₹12,00,000
  • Basic Salary (50%): ₹6,00,000 (₹50,000 / month)
  • Employer EPF (12% of Basic): ₹72,000 deducted from CTC
  • Gratuity Provision (4.81% of Basic): ₹28,860 retained by employer
  • Gross Cash Salary: ₹12,00,000 − ₹72,000 − ₹28,860 = ₹10,99,140 (₹91,595 / month)
  • Employee EPF Deduction (12%): ₹6,000 / month
  • Karnataka Professional Tax: ₹200 / month
  • Monthly Income Tax (New Regime TDS): ₹5,170 / month
  • Net In-Hand Bank Deposit: ₹91,595 − ₹6,000 − ₹200 − ₹5,170 = ₹80,225 / month

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I opt for the statutory PF cap of ₹1,800/month?

Under EPFO regulations, if your Basic Salary exceeds ₹15,000 per month, you and your employer can mutually agree to restrict provident fund contributions to 12% of the statutory wage limit of ₹15,000 (which equals ₹1,800 per month). This reduces your retirement accumulation but immediately boosts your monthly cash take-home by several thousand rupees.

Is variable pay or annual performance bonus included in monthly in-hand?

No. If your CTC package includes a 10% or 15% annual performance bonus or variable component, it is withheld by the employer and disbursed only once per year (or quarterly) subject to individual performance ratings and company profitability. It is taxed as bonus income in the month of disbursement.

What happens to Gratuity if I resign before 5 years?

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, gratuity is payable only upon completing at least 5 years of continuous service with the same organization. If you resign after 3 years, the 4.81% gratuity accrued in your CTC is forfeited and retained by the employer; it is not returned as cash.

What is Professional Tax and why is it ₹0 in Delhi?

Professional Tax (PT) is a state-level direct tax levied on salaried employees under Article 276(2) of the Indian Constitution, capped at ₹2,500 per financial year. States like Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, and West Bengal deduct approximately ₹200 per month. Delhi, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh do not levy any Professional Tax.

Which is better for a 15 LPA salary: Old or New Tax Regime?

For a 15 LPA CTC, the New Tax Regime yields approximately ₹99,400 monthly in-hand with ₹11,625 monthly TDS. To match this take-home under the Old Regime, you would need more than ₹3,85,000 in total deductions (combining 80C, 80D, and substantial HRA rent receipts). For most professionals without high rent in metro cities, the New Regime provides a higher in-hand salary.

How does the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction work under the New Tax Regime?

Budget 2024 increased the Standard Deduction under the New Tax Regime from ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 for salaried employees and pensioners. This deduction is applied automatically before calculating taxable income slabs, requiring no investment proofs, bills, or insurance receipts.

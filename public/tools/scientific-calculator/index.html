Educational Guide

PEMDAS / BODMAS Precedence & Left-Associative Evaluation

Mathematical expressions are evaluated strictly according to operator hierarchy. Multiplications and divisions do not precede one another; they share equal precedence and evaluate from left to right:

Expression: 8 ÷ 2 × (2 + 2)
Step 1 (Parentheses): 8 ÷ 2 × 4
Step 2 (Left-to-Right division before multiplication): 4 × 4 = 16 (Not 1!)

Always use explicit parentheses when authoring complex engineering denominators.

Angular Geometry: When Degrees Break Calculus

In elementary geometry, 360 degrees divide a circle arbitrarily based on ancient Babylonian astronomy. In mathematical analysis, radians measure angle as the ratio of arc length to radius ($s = r heta$):

sin(30°) = 0.5 (Degree Mode)
sin(30 rad) = -0.988031624 (Radian Mode)
Calculus derivatives (d/dx sin x = cos x) require radians. In degree mode, d/dx sin(x) = (π/180)cos(x).

Logarithms: Decibels, pH, and Continuous Compounding

Logarithms compress astronomical ranges into human-interpretable linear scales:

  • Common Log (log₁₀): Base 10. A sound of 80 dB carries 10 times more energy than 70 dB, and 100 times more than 60 dB.
  • Natural Log (ln, logₑ): Base e ≈ 2.71828. Used in thermodynamics, physics half-life formulas, and Black-Scholes financial options modeling.
  • Change of Base Formula: log_b(x) = ln(x) / ln(b).

IEEE 754 Binary Floating-Point: Why 0.1 + 0.2 ≠ 0.3

Modern computers represent numbers in base-2 (binary). Fractions like 1/10 (0.1) cannot be represented cleanly in binary, resulting in an infinite repeating fraction (similar to 1/3 = 0.3333... in decimal):

0.1 + 0.2 in raw JavaScript = 0.30000000000000004
LedgerMate's computational core applies precision epsilon clamping to ensure clean display.

Combinatorics: Permutations (Order Matters) vs. Combinations

Determining selection probabilities from finite sets:

Permutation nPr = n! ÷ (n - r)! → Selecting 1st, 2nd, 3rd place winners.
Combination nCr = n! ÷ [r!(n - r)!] → Selecting a committee of 3 people where order does not matter.

Complete Guide to Scientific Functions & Mathematical Conventions

Scientific calculators bridge the gap between basic arithmetic (+, −, ×, ÷) and complex engineering, physics, and calculus formulas. Below is a comprehensive reference to clarify every key and prevent common calculation errors.

1. Degree vs. Radian Mode: When to Use Which?

Angles can be measured in degrees (where a full circle is 360°) or radians (where a full circle is 2π radians ≈ 6.28318 radians). The relationship is defined by:

1 Radian = 180° / π ≈ 57.29578°
θ(radians) = θ(degrees) × (π / 180)
  • When to use Degrees: Architecture, navigation, geometry, and high school algebra. For example, sin(30°) = 0.5 and sin(90°) = 1.
  • When to use Radians: Advanced calculus, physics (harmonic motion, electromagnetic waves), and engineering. Derivatives of trigonometric functions (such as d/dx sin(x) = cos(x)) are only valid when x is expressed in radians.

2. Natural Log (ln) vs. Common Log (log)

  • log (Common Logarithm, base 10): Answers the question: "10 raised to what power equals x?" Used in measuring sound volume (Decibels dB), earthquake intensity (Richter scale), and chemical acidity (pH scale). For example, log(1000) = 3 because 10³ = 1000.
  • ln (Natural Logarithm, base e ≈ 2.71828): Answers: "e raised to what power equals x?" Essential for radioactive decay, continuous compound interest, population growth models, and thermodynamics. For example, ln(e) = 1 and ln(1) = 0.

3. Why 0! (Zero Factorial) Equals 1

A factorial (n!) is the product of all positive integers from 1 down to n. For instance, 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24. Why is 0! = 1 and not 0?

  • Combinatorial reasoning: n! represents how many ways you can arrange n distinct objects in a line. How many ways can you arrange zero items? Exactly 1 way: do nothing (the empty set).
  • Algebraic definition: Notice the pattern: n! = (n+1)! / (n+1). If n = 0, then 0! = 1! / 1 = 1 / 1 = 1.

4. Order of Operations (PEMDAS / BODMAS)

Expressions typed into LedgerMate are evaluated in strict accordance with mathematical precedence rules:

  1. P / B: Parentheses & Brackets (expressions inside ( ) take utmost priority)
  2. E / O: Exponents & Orders (powers x^y and square roots √x)
  3. MD / DM: Multiplication and Division (evaluated from left to right)
  4. AS: Addition and Subtraction (evaluated from left to right)

Example: 2 + 3 × 4 equals 14 (not 20), because multiplication takes precedence over addition. To force addition first, enter (2 + 3) × 4 = 20.

Why does 0.1 + 0.2 equal 0.30000000000000004 on computers?

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Clear answers to common scientific calculation, syntax, and accuracy questions.

What is the difference between DEG and RAD mode?

DEG (Degrees) treats a complete circle as 360 degrees, commonly used in navigation and basic geometry. RAD (Radians) treats a circle as 2π (≈ 6.28318) radians, which is mandatory for calculus, physics, and advanced trigonometry because trigonometric derivatives are only mathematically valid in radians.

Why does 0! (zero factorial) equal 1?

Combinatorially, n! represents the number of ways to arrange n distinct objects. There is exactly one way to arrange zero objects: the empty set. Algebraically, because n! = (n+1)! / (n+1), setting n = 0 yields 0! = 1! / 1 = 1.

What is the difference between log and ln?

log denotes the common logarithm with base 10 (asking "10 to what power equals x?"), commonly used in decibels, Richter scales, and pH. ln is the natural logarithm with base e (≈ 2.71828), which governs radioactive decay, compound interest, and physics models.

How does the calculator handle order of operations (PEMDAS)?

Expressions are evaluated following standard mathematical precedence: Parentheses first, Exponents and roots second, Multiplication and Division third (from left to right), and Addition and Subtraction last (from left to right). For example, 2 + 3 * 4 = 14, not 20.

Why does 0.1 + 0.2 equal 0.30000000000000004 in binary computing?

Computers represent numbers using IEEE 754 binary floating-point. In base-2, the fraction 1/10 is an infinitely repeating sequence, resulting in a microscopic rounding error. LedgerMate automatically formats and rounds results to prevent this display artifact.

How do memory keys (MC, MR, M+, M-) work?

M+ adds the current display value to the stored memory register. M- subtracts it. MR (Memory Recall) displays the stored number without clearing it. MC (Memory Clear) resets the memory register to zero.

How to Calculate Prorated Rent

The definitive mathematical formulas, tenant rights guidelines, and practical examples for mid-month move-ins in the US, Canada, and Europe.

1. What Is Prorated Rent?

Prorated rent is the adjusted amount of rent a tenant pays when moving into or out of a rental unit partway through a billing cycle. Instead of paying for a full 30- or 31-day month, you only pay for the exact days you have legal access to the property.

2. The Prorated Rent Formula

Calculating prorated rent requires two simple arithmetic steps:

  1. Step 1: Calculate the Daily Rental Rate
    Daily Rate = Monthly Rent ÷ Total Days in Month
  2. Step 2: Multiply by Days of Occupancy
    Prorated Rent = Daily Rate × Days Occupied

Real-World Example: Move-In on October 18

  • Monthly Rent: $2,400
  • Days in October: 31 days
  • Move-In Date: October 18
  • Occupancy Calculation: 31 - 18 + 1 = 14 billable days (remember, moving in on Oct 18 means you occupy the property on Oct 18, so you add 1).
  • Daily Rate: $2,400 ÷ 31 = $77.419 per day
  • Prorated Rent Due: $77.419 × 14 = $1,083.87

3. The 3 Proration Methods Used by Landlords

Different landlords and property management companies use different divisors to establish daily rental rates:

Method A: Exact Days in Month (Most Common & Fair)

This method uses the actual calendar days of the move-in month (28, 29, 30, or 31). It is the most legally defensible method in tenant courts across California, New York, Texas, and Ontario.

Method B: The Banker's 30-Day Rule

Commercial leases and some older residential leases treat every month as exactly 30 days. Under this method, a $2,400 apartment has a fixed daily rate of $2,400 ÷ 30 = $80.00/day, regardless of whether the month is February or July.

Method C: Annualized 365-Day Method

Large national property management corporations (like Greystar, AvalonBay, and Equity Residential) often use an annual calculation to streamline accounting across 12-month lease agreements:

Daily Rate = (Monthly Rent × 12) ÷ 365

For a $2,400/month apartment: ($28,800 ÷ 365) = $78.90 per day.

4. What About Prorating Utilities & Roommates?

If you share an apartment with roommates and someone moves out on the 15th, you should also prorate the shared utilities:

  • Fixed Utilities (Internet): Prorate using the exact same day ratio as rent.
  • Variable Utilities (Water, Electric, Gas): If the utility bill arrives after the billing cycle ends, calculate the person's daily share based on their move-out date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you pay for move-in day when prorating?

Yes. You receive access to the premises on that date, which makes it a billable occupancy day. The calculation is always (Days in Month - Move-In Date + 1).

When is prorated rent paid?

Typically, landlords require prorated rent to be paid on or before the day you pick up keys, along with your security deposit. On the 1st of the following month, you resume paying the normal full monthly rent.

Can you prorate rent on a lease renewal?

Proration is standard anytime a tenancy begins or terminates on a date other than the first or last day of the calendar month.

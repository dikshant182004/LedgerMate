How to Split Rent Fairly When Room Sizes Are Unequal

Updated 2026 · 5 min read

You and your future roommates just found the ideal 3-bedroom apartment. There’s just one catch: Bedroom A is a massive 220-sq-ft master suite with an ensuite bathroom and walk-in closet. Bedroom B is an average 140-sq-ft bedroom. Bedroom C is a modest 100-sq-ft room that barely fits a full bed and a desk.

Dividing the $3,000 monthly rent equally at $1,000 each is technically "equal", but it isn't fair. Within two months, Roommate C will feel taken advantage of, and roommate tension will brew. Here is how to divide rent mathematically and peacefully.

Method 1: The 50/50 Common Space Split (Most Recommended)

The most common rookie mistake is dividing 100% of the rent strictly by bedroom square footage. That ignores that everyone shares the kitchen, living room, laundry, and hallways equally.

The Rule of Thumb: 50% of the rent pays for the shared common areas (divided evenly by headcount). 50% of the rent pays for the private bedrooms (divided by square footage or room weight).

Step-by-Step Example:

Suppose total apartment rent is $3,000 for 3 roommates:

  1. Common Areas (50% = $1,500): Each roommate contributes $500 toward the shared kitchen, living room, and balcony.
  2. Private Bedrooms (50% = $1,500): Distributed by square footage:
    • Room A (220 sq ft = 48% of bedroom space): $720
    • Room B (140 sq ft = 30% of bedroom space): $450
    • Room C (100 sq ft = 22% of bedroom space): $330
  3. Final Rent:
    • Roommate A: $500 + $720 = $1,220
    • Roommate B: $500 + $450 = $950
    • Roommate C: $500 + $330 = $830

Method 2: The Amenity Multiplier System

Square footage alone doesn't account for perks like private balconies or ensuite bathrooms. If measuring tape sounds like too much work, use standard amenity weights:

  • Base Room (Shared bath, standard closet): Weight 1.0
  • Private Attached Bathroom: Add +0.25 to +0.35 weight
  • Walk-in Closet: Add +0.10 weight
  • Private Balcony or Patio: Add +0.10 weight
  • Basement Room / Low Natural Light: Subtract -0.15 weight

Method 3: The Secret Roommate Auction

If two roommates want the master bedroom and can't agree on who gets it, let market forces decide via a friendly blind auction:

  1. Everyone writes down on a piece of paper the maximum amount they would be willing to pay for each room, ensuring their total bids equal the lease price.
  2. Assign each room to the highest bidder for that room.
  3. The person who values the master suite the most gets it, and their higher payment lowers everyone else’s monthly obligation.

How to Handle Utilities & Household Supplies

While bedroom rent should be weighted, utilities (Wi-Fi, electric, gas, water, trash) should almost always be split 100% evenly. A roommate in a small room uses just as much hot water, refrigerator space, and Wi-Fi bandwidth as the person in the master suite.

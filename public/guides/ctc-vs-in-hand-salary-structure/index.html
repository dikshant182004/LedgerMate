The Anatomy of an Offer Letter: CTC vs. Gross vs. Net In-Hand

Every year, millions of Indian professionals sign employment contracts celebrating a new annual package—say, ₹12,00,000 (12 LPA)—expecting a monthly bank deposit of ₹1,00,000. When their first paycheck arrives and displays ₹82,410, confusion and disappointment follow. Where did the missing ₹17,590 go?

The discrepancy lies in the three fundamentally different definitions of compensation used in modern payroll:

  • Cost to Company (CTC): The total annual expenditure the company spends to keep you on payroll. It includes not only what you get paid, but also employer retirement matching, statutory insurance reserves, group health policies, and cafeteria subsidies.
  • Gross Salary: Your total earnings before mandatory personal deductions (Employee EPF, Professional Tax, and Income Tax TDS). Gross Salary equals CTC minus employer-side non-cash allocations.
  • Net In-Hand (Take-Home) Salary: The actual liquid cash deposited into your salary savings bank account on the last working day of the month.

Step 1: The Deductions Taken From CTC to Reach Gross

Before your monthly paycheck is even drafted, your employer carves out mandatory employer provisions from your headline CTC:

Component Standard Percentage Why It Is Subtracted From CTC
Employer EPF 12% of Basic Pay Deposited directly into your EPFO retirement ledger. You cannot touch this money until retirement or job transitions.
Gratuity Reserve 4.81% of Basic Pay (15/26 days) Required by the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972. Paid only if you complete 5 continuous years at the firm.
Group Health Insurance ₹10,000 – ₹25,000 flat / yr Corporate medical cover premium paid to insurer on your behalf.
Variable / Performance Bonus 10% – 20% of CTC Disbursed once per year subject to corporate and personal KPIs; not paid in monthly salary slips.

Step 2: The Deductions Taken From Gross to Reach In-Hand Pay

Once your Gross Monthly Pay is established, the finance department applies mandatory personal statutory deductions:

  1. Employee EPF (12% of Basic): Matches the employer's contribution and goes into your provident fund account earning a government-backed interest rate (~8.25% p.a.).
  2. Professional Tax (PT): A state tax of ₹200 per month (in Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, and West Bengal). Delhi, Haryana, and UP levy zero PT.
  3. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS): Advance monthly installment of your annual income tax liability under the New Tax Regime (default) or Old Tax Regime.

The Math Example: Breakdown of a 12 LPA CTC Offer

Let us trace a real-world ₹12,00,000 (12 LPA) salary structured under standard Indian corporate norms (50% Basic salary, New Tax Regime, Karnataka location):

  • Annual CTC: ₹12,00,000
  • Basic Salary (50%): ₹6,00,000/yr (₹50,000/mo)
  • Employer EPF (12% of Basic): ₹72,000/yr (₹6,000/mo)
  • Gratuity Reserve (4.81% of Basic): ₹28,860/yr (₹2,405/mo)
  • Annual Gross Salary: ₹12,00,000 − ₹72,000 − ₹28,860 = ₹10,99,140 (₹91,595/mo)
  • Employee EPF Deduction: ₹6,000/mo
  • Professional Tax (Karnataka): ₹200/mo
  • New Regime Income Tax TDS: ₹6,380/mo (approx)
  • Final Monthly In-Hand Pay: ₹91,595 − ₹6,000 − ₹200 − ₹6,380 = ₹79,015

Effective cash take-home rate: 79.0% of the headline CTC letter.

40% vs. 50% Basic Salary: Which Should You Choose?

Many Indian technology and multinational companies allow employees to select their basic salary percentage upon onboarding:

  • 40% Basic: Results in lower EPF deductions (12% × 40% = 4.8% of CTC). You have higher immediate cash in hand for rent, EMI, or index fund SIPs, but a smaller retirement corpus.
  • 50% Basic: Standard benchmark compliant with the Code on Wages guidelines. Results in higher EPF savings, higher tax deductions under Section 80C (if using the Old Regime), and higher statutory gratuity upon leaving.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can employers force you to keep 12% PF on uncapped basic pay?

Under Supreme Court precedents and EPFO rules, statutory PF is only mandatory up to a basic salary of ₹15,000 per month (₹1,800/month cap). Many private companies allow employees to restrict PF contributions to the statutory ₹1,800 limit, returning the difference as Special Allowance in your monthly cash pay.

How is Gratuity calculated when you resign?

Under the Payment of Gratuity Act, the formula is: Gratuity = (15 × Last Drawn Basic Salary × Number of Completed Years) / 26. Six months or more in the final year is rounded up to a full year. Gratuity up to ₹20,00,000 is 100% tax-exempt.

What is Special Allowance and why is it fully taxable?

Special Allowance is a balancing component in your salary slip that absorbs the remainder of your CTC after basic pay, HRA, and employer contributions are computed. Unlike HRA or LTA, Special Allowance offers zero tax exemptions under Indian tax law and is 100% taxable at your highest slab rate.

What happens if my company delays depositing my EPF?

Employers are legally obligated to remit both employee and employer EPF contributions to the EPFO by the 15th of the following month. You can verify timely monthly deposits using the EPFO Passbook portal or the UMANG app.

Why is New Tax Regime usually better than Old Tax Regime in 2026?

The New Regime features reduced tax rates, zero tax up to ₹7.75 Lakhs with the ₹75,000 standard deduction and Section 87A rebate. Unless your aggregate deductions under 80C, 80D, HRA, and Home Loan interest exceed ₹3.75 Lakhs to ₹4 Lakhs, the New Regime provides a higher monthly in-hand bank credit.

Do I have to pay Professional Tax if I work remotely?

Generally, professional tax is determined by the state where your employer's registered payroll entity or operating branch is located, or where your primary contract of employment is domiciled.

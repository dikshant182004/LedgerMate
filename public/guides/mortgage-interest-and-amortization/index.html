Mortgage Amortization & Opportunity Cost Explained

Understand how front-loaded bank interest works, why early equity builds so slowly, and how the opportunity cost of your down payment shapes the rent vs. buy decision.

1. Front-Loaded Mortgage Interest: How Amortization Works

When you take out a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage, your monthly payment remains identical from month 1 to month 360. However, the internal distribution between principal paydown and interest paid to the bank changes drastically over time.

Monthly Interest Equation:
Interest Due That Month = Remaining Loan Principal × (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12)
Principal Reduction = Monthly Fixed Payment - Interest Due That Month

Because your loan balance is at its absolute maximum on day one, the interest charge is at its peak. On a $400,000 mortgage at 6.5% interest:

  • Month 1: Total payment is $2,528. The bank takes $2,167 in interest (85.7%), and only $361 goes toward your home equity (14.3%).
  • Month 60 (Year 5): Total payment is $2,528. Interest is still $1,974 (78.1%), and principal paydown is only $554.
  • Month 180 (Year 15): Interest drops to $1,348 (53.3%), with principal repayment reaching $1,180.

2. Down Payment & The Opportunity Cost of Capital

When you purchase a $500,000 home with a 20% down payment, you write a non-recoverable check for $100,000. That capital is now locked in residential real estate equity. It earns no dividends and cannot be accessed without paying thousands in refinancing costs or selling the property.

3. The Price-to-Rent (P/R) Ratio & The 5% Rule

The Price-to-Rent ratio is calculated by dividing the purchase price of a home by the total annual rent of a comparable rental property:

Price-to-Rent Ratio = Home Purchase Price ÷ (Monthly Rent × 12)
  • P/R under 15: Real estate prices are reasonable relative to rental income; buying is financially favored.
  • P/R 16 to 20: Balanced neutral territory. Decision depends on personal timeline (staying 7+ years favors buying).
  • P/R 21+: Real estate prices have detached from rental yields; renting and investing the difference builds superior net worth.

The 5% Rule: Sum the three unrecoverable costs of homeownership: property taxes (~1%), maintenance (~1%), and cost of capital (~3%). Multiply the home value by 5% and divide by 12. If comparable monthly rent is less than this threshold, renting is the mathematically superior choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is mortgage interest front-loaded in the early years?

Because monthly interest is calculated as outstanding loan principal multiplied by monthly interest rate. In Year 1, when the principal balance is at its maximum, the interest charge is at its highest point, absorbing up to 80% of each monthly mortgage payment.

What is the opportunity cost of a down payment?

The opportunity cost is the investment return you forfeit by locking liquid cash into home equity rather than keeping it invested in productive assets like diversified global stock index funds, which historically return 8% to 10% annually.

What is the 5% Rule in real estate?

The 5% Rule estimates total unrecoverable costs of homeownership: 1% for property taxes, 1% for maintenance, and 3% for the cost of capital. If equivalent annual rent is less than 5% of the purchase price, renting is mathematically superior.

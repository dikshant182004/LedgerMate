How Prorated Rent Is Calculated: The 4 Methods

1. The Four Statutory Proration Divisors

When a tenant moves into or out of an apartment mid-month, prorated rent charges only for the exact days of physical possession. Across global rental jurisdictions, four primary divisors are used:

Method Name Daily Rate Formula Where It Is Commonly Used
1. Exact Calendar Days (Most Common) Monthly Rent ÷ Days in Move-In Month Standard across US, Canada, UK & Germany
2. 30-Day Banker's Rule Monthly Rent ÷ 30 Institutional commercial leases & corporate landlords
3. Annual 365-Day Method (Monthly Rent × 12) ÷ 365 UK Assured Shorthold Tenancies & corporate relocation
4. Weekly Rent Rule (AU/NZ) Weekly Rent ÷ 7 Statutory legal standard across Australia & New Zealand

2. The Move-In Day Rule (Inclusive vs Exclusive)

A frequent point of dispute is whether the move-in day itself is billed. Under residential tenancy laws across the United States (California Civil Code § 1946, New York Real Property Law), the United Kingdom, and Canada, the tenant receives key access on the move-in date. Therefore, the move-in day is counted as a full occupied day.

Formula for Occupied Days:
Occupied Days = Total Days in Month - Move-In Day + 1
Example: Moving in on October 10 (31-day month): 31 - 10 + 1 = 22 occupied days.

3. Security Deposits & Advance Rent Rules

In most residential leases, the prorated first month is paid along with the security deposit at lease signing. Many institutional landlords prefer collecting a full first month's rent at move-in and applying the prorated discount to the second month of occupancy to protect against immediate defaults.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the move-in day counted when calculating prorated rent?

Yes, under standard property management law in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia, the move-in day itself is an occupied day and is included in the total days charged. If you move in on September 15, you pay for 16 days (September 15 through 30 inclusive).

What is the difference between Exact Days and 30-Day Banker's Rule?

Exact Days divides monthly rent by the actual number of calendar days in that specific month (28, 29, 30, or 31). The 30-Day Banker's rule assumes every month has exactly 30 days regardless of the calendar, which simplifies accounting but can create slight variations in February or 31-day months.

How does prorated rent work in Australia and New Zealand?

In Australia and NZ, leases are quoted as weekly rent. To calculate the daily rate, weekly rent is divided by 7 days. Monthly rent is derived using the statutory formula: (Weekly Rent × 52) ÷ 12.

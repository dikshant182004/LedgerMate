Introduction: Why 35% to 45% of Your German Salary Disappears

For expatriates and international professionals moving to Germany, the first payslip (Gehaltsabrechnung) is notoriously difficult to decode. A contract promising €60,000 Brutto yields approximately €3,100 Netto per month in bank deposits. Where did the other €1,900 go?

In Germany, statutory deductions are split into two major pillars:

  1. Income Tax (Lohnsteuer) & Surcharges: Progressive income tax determined by your designated Steuerklasse (Tax Class), plus optional Church Tax (Kirchensteuer) and the Solidarity Surcharge (Solidaritätszuschlag) for top earners.
  2. Social Security Contributions (Sozialversicherungsbeiträge): Approximately 40% of total wages, shared 50/50 equally between you and your employer for healthcare, state pension, unemployment, and nursing home care.

The 6 German Tax Classes (Steuerklassen) Explained in Simple English

Your tax class dictates how much tax is automatically withheld by your employer's payroll software each month. It is assigned based on marital and household status:

Class Target Group Key Features & Withholding Profile
Steuerklasse I Single / Unmarried / Divorced / Widowed Standard basic tax-free allowance (Grundfreibetrag ~€11,784). No spouse splitting. Highest baseline single rate.
Steuerklasse II Single Parents (Alleinerziehende) Includes an additional single-parent relief allowance (Entlastungsbetrag of €4,260/yr) to reduce tax burden.
Steuerklasse III Married — Primary High Earner Double the basic tax-free allowance. Low tax withholding. Paired with spouse in Class V. Ideal if earning 60%+ of total.
Steuerklasse IV Married — Equal Earners Automatic default upon marriage in Germany. Identical to Class I withholding, but eligible for annual joint return splitting.
Steuerklasse V Married — Secondary Earner Zero basic tax-free allowance (transferred to Class III spouse). High withholding on secondary paycheck.
Steuerklasse VI Second Job / Side Employment Zero allowances. Every euro is taxed from cent one. Used for multiple simultaneous employment contracts.

Essential German Payroll Vocabulary (Glossary for Expats)

When reviewing your German payslip (Lohnabrechnung), keep this cheat sheet handy:

German Term English Translation Simple Meaning
Bruttogehalt Gross Salary The headline total compensation before any taxes or social fees are subtracted.
Nettogehalt / Auszahlungsbetrag Net Salary / Payout Amount The actual liquid euros transferred to your IBAN checking account.
Lohnsteuer Wage Tax / Income Tax Income tax withheld directly by your employer and paid to the Finanzamt.
Kirchensteuer Church Tax 8% (BY/BW) or 9% surcharge on your income tax for registered religious members.
Solidaritätszuschlag (Soli) Solidarity Surcharge Originally levied for German reunification; now only paid by the top 10% of earners.
Beitragsbemessungsgrenze Contribution Ceiling Max gross income subject to social insurance. Earnings above this threshold are social-fee-free.
Werbungskostenpauschale Employee Expense Allowance Automatic €1,230 annual tax deduction granted to all employees for work-related expenses.
Pendlerpauschale Commuter Allowance €0.30 to €0.38 per kilometer of distance from home to workplace deductible on tax returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can expats claim back German pension contributions when leaving Germany?

If you are a citizen of a non-EU / non-treaty country (e.g. India, USA) and you contributed to the German state pension (DRV) for less than 60 months (5 years), you can apply for a full refund of your employee contributions (9.3%) 24 months after permanently moving outside the EU.

Is it true that Steuerklasse 3 and 5 creates tax debts (Nachzahlung)?

Yes. The 3/5 combination minimizes monthly withholding, but when you file your mandatory joint tax return, the tax office calculates the exact household tax owed. If the higher earner had bonuses or variable compensation, you may face a back-tax bill of €500 to €2,000. Steuerklasse IV with factor avoids this surprise.

What is private health insurance (PKV) vs public (GKV)?

If your annual gross income exceeds the Versicherungspflichtgrenze (~€69,300/year), you can legally opt out of statutory public healthcare (GKV) and purchase Private Health Insurance (PKV). PKV premiums depend on your age and health rather than income, which can save high earners substantial money, though premiums rise in retirement.

How do I change my Steuerklasse in Germany?

You can change your tax class once per year (or upon marriage/separation) online through the official ELSTER tax portal (elster.de) or by submitting the paper form Antrag auf Steuerklassenwechsel to your local tax office (Finanzamt).

Does having children reduce taxes in Germany?

Yes. Parents receive either monthly tax-free Child Benefit (Kindergeld, €250/month per child) or the Child Tax Allowance (Kinderfreibetrag), whichever saves more money. Parents are also exempt from the 0.6% long-term care insurance childless surcharge.

What is the mini-job threshold in Germany?

A Mini-Job has an earnings limit of €538 per month. Income earned within a mini-job is completely tax-free and exempt from social security contributions for the employee.
